BDR basics

What a Business Development Representative (BDR) does for an AI agency

Usman Amer · Business Development Representative · 13 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

A Business Development Representative (BDR) finds the companies that need what you sell, starts the conversation, and hands your team a qualified meeting. For an AI or software agency, that one role decides whether your developers spend next month building or waiting.

The short answer

A BDR owns the top of your sales pipeline. They research who to talk to, reach out, qualify the people who reply, and book the first real call. In a small agency, the BDR often also writes proposals and runs discovery, because there is no separate closer yet.

Where a BDR fits in the funnel

Think of an agency's sales process as four stages:

  1. Find: build a list of companies that match your ideal client.
  2. Start: reach out with a message that gives them a reason to reply.
  3. Qualify: check that the lead has budget, authority, need and a timeline (BANT).
  4. Close: discovery, proposal, negotiation and a signed contract.

The BDR runs the first three stages. An account executive or the founder usually takes the fourth. As a BDR grows into a technical closer, they take on more of stage four too.

What the day-to-day work looks like

Sourcing leads

For AI and software agencies, good leads come from a few places: live projects on Upwork, filtered lists in LinkedIn Sales Navigator and Apollo.io, and intent signals such as a company hiring for a role your service replaces, or a founder asking for help on Reddit.

Outreach

Personal LinkedIn messages, cold email sequences and Upwork proposals. The best first message mentions something specific about the prospect and asks one easy question. Generic templates get ignored.

Qualification

Not every reply is a lead. A BDR checks the basics early so your team never spends an hour on a call with someone who has no budget or can't sign.

Booking and handover

Qualified leads get a discovery call on the calendar, with notes in the CRM: who they are, what they need, and what was promised. A clean handover is half the job.

Keeping the pipeline honest

Every lead, follow-up and next step lives in a CRM such as HubSpot, Apollo or ClickUp. If it isn't logged, it didn't happen.

BDR vs SDR vs account executive

  • SDR (Sales Development Representative): often focused on inbound leads, the people who already raised their hand.
  • BDR (Business Development Representative): often focused on outbound, finding and starting conversations with people who haven't heard of you yet.
  • Account executive (AE): takes qualified meetings and closes the deal.

In small agencies these titles blur. One person frequently does all three, which is why many agencies hire for "SDR/BDR" as one role.

Why AI and software agencies need a technical BDR

Selling AI automation or custom software is different from selling a simple product. Prospects ask how it integrates with their CRM, whether it will hallucinate, how long setup takes. A BDR who understands the basics of APIs, automation tools and MVP scoping can answer early questions with confidence and bring engineers into the call at the right moment, not the first one.

What to look for when hiring a BDR

  • A clear process for finding leads, not just "I send a lot of messages".
  • Comfort with your channels: Upwork, LinkedIn, email.
  • Writing that sounds human and specific.
  • Discipline with the CRM.
  • Enough technical understanding to scope with your developers.
